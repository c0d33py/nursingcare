## 2. Voice, tone & style

### 2.1 Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Home hero, CTA bands | Warm, confident | "The care they need, in the home they love." |
| Service pages | Clear, precise, reassuring | "We need a valid prescription for all injections and IV medication." |
| FAQ | Direct, honest | "No. We are not an emergency service." |
| Forms and errors | Simple, blame-free | "Please enter a phone number we can reach you on." |
| Overseas page | Empathetic, practical | "You can't be there every day. We can." |
| Hospitals page | Professional, concise | "Share the discharge summary and orders; we confirm staffing." |

### 2.2 Writing rules
1. **Lead with the patient or family benefit, then the clinical substance.**
2. **Short sentences** (average ≤ 18 words), short paragraphs (≤ 3 sentences), aiming for a reading level around grade 7–8.
3. **Explain medical terms the first time they appear on a page:** "a nasogastric (NG) tube — a thin, soft tube passed through the nose into the stomach".
4. **Second person ("you", "your loved one") and first-person plural ("we").** Active voice.
5. **Sentence case** for all headings and buttons ("Book a home visit"). The brand name keeps its own capitals. Never use ALL CAPS in body text.
6. **British English spelling** (colour, centre, programme), consistent with Pakistani usage. "-ize" endings are acceptable to match the source (Oxford style), but be consistent within a page.
7. **Numbers:** numerals for all numbers ("3 steps", "24/7"). Times as "8 am–8 pm". Phone as **0324 4744447** for local readers and **+92 324 4744447** on the overseas page and in the schema.
8. **"24/7" always says what is available 24/7** ("Our phone and WhatsApp line is open 24/7"). Never let it imply emergency response.
9. **One idea per heading.** Headings should make sense when read on their own (screen readers and skimmers).
10. **No unverifiable superlatives.** Prefer specifics: "qualified nurses for IV medication" over "expert medical care".

### 2.3 Terminology

| Use | Avoid / note |
|---|---|
| **qualified nurse** (registered nurse) | Calling assistant nurses or caregivers "nurses" |
| **assistant nurse** | — |
| **caregiver (aya)**. On first mention per page write "caregiver (aya)", then "caregiver". | Using "maid" or "servant" |
| **patient attendant**: an SEO synonym for caregiver; use once or twice where natural | Overusing it in headings |
| **patient** in clinical contexts; **your loved one** or **your parent** in emotional contexts | "Client" or "customer" for the patient |
| **home visit**, **shift** (day, night, 24-hour) | "Package" (sounds like pricing) |
| **care coordinator** [CONFIRM the role exists] | Inventing job titles |
| **drip** (alongside "IV fluids") because families search for it | Only clinical wording |
| **bed sores (pressure sores)** | — |
| **Rescue 1122** for emergencies | — |

### 2.4 Words and patterns to avoid
- **Claims:** "best in Lahore", "No. 1", "guaranteed", "100% safe", "cure", "hospital-grade", "instant", "always available within X minutes" (unless operationally true and verified), "cheap", "lowest price".
- **Name puns:** "heaven" relates to death and the afterlife. **Never** play on the name near illness, elderly care or end-of-life topics ("heaven-sent", "a touch of heaven", "angels watching over"). Let "Heaven" mean peace and comfort through design, not wordplay.
- **Fear-based copy:** "Don't let your parents suffer alone." Use reassurance, not guilt.
- **Overly clinical imagery in words:** describe procedures matter-of-factly. No graphic descriptions of wounds or bodily fluids.
- **Patient-identifying stories** without written consent.

## 9. Testimonials & reviews

**Rule: never write, invent or "improve" testimonials.** Only lightly trim them, with the author's approval. Unreal testimonials are dishonest, and in health care they're dangerous.

### 9.1 Collection process
1. About 2 weeks into care (or at the end of short-term care), the coordinator asks by WhatsApp: "Would you share a few words about your experience? We'd love to put it on our website."
2. Prompts: "What was the situation? How did our team help? How did it make your family feel?"
3. **Written consent** (WhatsApp confirmation is acceptable) covering the website, social media and how the person will be named.
4. Also send a Google review link (short URL or QR code) for Google Business Profile.
5. Store the original text, consent and date in the content repository (doc 04 §3.16).

### 9.2 Display format
- 30–60 words; specific (service, situation, outcome feeling).
- **Attribution:** first name + initial, relationship and area. Example format: *"[Quote]" — Ayesha K., daughter of a patient, Johar Town* (format only; not a real testimonial).
- Photos only with explicit consent. No patient medical details beyond what the author wrote and approved.
- Testimonials written in Urdu: show the English translation with "Translated from Urdu" (and the original in the Urdu site, Phase 2).
- Tag each testimonial with a service, so the right ones appear on S-pages.

### 9.3 Placement
P01 (3–6), relevant S-pages (1–2), P10 (overseas families), P03 (about staff). **If there are fewer than 3 real testimonials at launch, leave the section out** and link to Google reviews instead.
